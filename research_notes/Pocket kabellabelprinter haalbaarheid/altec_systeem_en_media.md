## 2. What exactly are the yellow labels?

### Takeaway
The yellow labels are most likely Altec "ADC Kabelmerkers": flat, non-adhesive, halogen-free, flame-retardant (UL 94 V-0) plastic tags of material code PU3500 (polyurethane-type), typically 60 x 10 mm, supplied as die-cut tags on a roll, printed black-on-yellow by thermal transfer and fixed to the cable with two tie-wraps. Siblings are the low-smoke PU3501 version (60 x 12 mm, 500/roll), ADC Single Fix (40 x 14.4 mm), WrapTags (100 x 18 mm, 500/roll, self-fastening) and the Brady B-7643 tags Altec also sells.

### Inferences
- The user's "yellow Altec labels" are with high probability ADC Kabelmerkers PU3500, yellow, 60 x 10 mm (article I016652); 60 x 10 mm is the de-facto Dutch standard size for tie-on cable markers (same size at Altec ADC, Altec THT HCM, Brady B-7643, Partex PPQZ, Cembre ETF-ROLL, Electroplast). Second candidates: low-smoke 60 x 12 mm, WrapTags, or the 75 x 25 mm ZZ3602 format.
- Supply form is a roll of individual pre-cut rigid tags (with tie-wrap slots/holes at both ends and a gap or notch between tags for the sensor), fed through a 4-inch printer in landscape orientation. The printer's "notch, gap and black-mark" sensors are what registers them. Roll width is therefore about 60 mm (one tag across) for the 60 x 10 format; this is inferred, the extracts do not state web width, pitch or core diameter.
- These are tags, not adhesive labels and not sleeves: no adhesive, no liner to peel in the usual sense, thickness in the order of 0.5 mm (by analogy with Cembre MG-ETF at 0.5 mm; Altec's own thickness not found). A pocket printer would have to transport stiff ~0.5 mm stock, which rules out most tape-cassette label makers.
- "1000 per roll, external roll holder required" for the THT HCM marker shows roll outer diameter exceeds what a compact desktop printer holds internally - a roll of 500-1000 rigid tags is physically large (order of 150-200 mm diameter, inferred), so a pocket device can only carry a short strip or small cassette (tens of tags).
- At about EUR 0.76 per 75 x 25 mm tag, a 60 x 10 mm tag is probably in the EUR 0.20-0.50 range (estimate, no price found).

- Exact geometry: slot/hole positions, tag pitch, gap or notch between tags, carrier/liner or linerless, roll core diameter, roll outer diameter, tags per roll for the 60 x 10 mm PU3500 version.

## 3. Print technology, ribbons, resolution, content and durability

### Takeaway
The system is thermal transfer with a black ribbon (Altec AR-01, 110 mm x 300 m; AR-98 as alternative) at 300 dpi on a 106 mm wide printhead; direct thermal is not usable on these tags. Durability claims in the extracts are limited to halogen-free, UL 94 V-0 and (low-smoke version) EN 45545-2 HL3; UV resistance outdoors is not inherent - Altec recommends a transparent UV laminate.

### Cited Findings
- ATP-300 Pro prints thermal transfer and direct thermal, 300 dpi (12 dots/mm), max print width 106 mm, ribbon length 300 m — [ATP-300 Pro flyer](https://files.altec.nl/20240314091327/ATP-300Pro_-_A4-flyer.pdf)
- AR-01: black ribbon 110 mm x 300 m, usable on ATP-300 Pro, ATP-600 Pro and ATP-3000; AR-01 is the recommended ribbon for ADC cable markers, ADC Single Fix, industrial vinyl and ResoPrint — [altec.nl AR-01](https://altec.nl/en/ink-ribbons-cartridges/product/ar-01/); [altec.be ADC Kabelmerkers](https://altec.be/labels/kabelcodering/product/adc-kabelmerkers/)
- AR-98 is named as the alternative recommended ribbon for ADC cable markers — [altec.be ADC kabelmerker oranje](https://altec.be/labels/kabelcodering/product/adc-kabelmerkers/adc-060x010-pu3500or-adc-kabelmerker-oranje-60x10mm/)

- Data source for text: cable lists imported from Excel/ASCII into DataPrint or PrintLabels.Online, serial numbering supported — [altec.be software blog](https://altec.be/blog/zelf-labels-ontwerpen-of-geautomatiseerd-printen/); [altec.nl PrintLabels.Online](https://altec.nl/en/software/product/printlabels-online/)
- Throughput benchmark from a competitor: Partex states 60 x 10 mm yellow halogen-free flat markers print at up to 2000 pieces/hour on its MK10 thermal transfer printer — [Partex Direct PPQZ](https://www.partex-direct.co.uk/yellow-halogen-free-60x10mm-flat-cable-marker-ppqz10060dn4)

### Inferences
- AR-01 on plastic tags is almost certainly a resin (or resin-enhanced) ribbon; wax ribbons do not adhere durably to polyurethane/polyolefin tags. The ribbon type of AR-01/AR-98 is not stated in the extracts.
- Required print area for a 60 x 10 mm tag is about 10 mm high x 35-45 mm long (tie slots take the ends; Cembre's 13 x 57 mm tag has an 11 x 35 mm print area). A pocket printer therefore needs only a 10-15 mm wide printhead (about 120-180 dots at 300 dpi), versus the 106 mm head of the ATP-300 Pro; 25 mm would cover the 75 x 25 mm format.
- Typical content is one line of a cable number/code such as "W101", "-W12.3" or a 10-20 character tag ID, optionally 2 lines; 203 dpi is legible for this (Phoenix Contact's mobile unit uses 203 dpi), 300 dpi is the incumbent standard.
- The ribbon is a mandatory second consumable: a pocket device needs a narrow resin ribbon cassette (about 12-25 mm wide) and a head able to deliver resin-level energy to a stiff, heat-sinking 0.5 mm substrate - the main technical difference from direct-thermal pocket label printers.
- Ribbon economics on the desktop: a 110 mm wide ribbon is consumed over the full 60 mm tag length even though print is only 10 mm high; a narrow ribbon would waste far less.

### Gaps
- Ribbon chemistry and price of AR-01/AR-98; narrower ribbon widths offered by Altec.
- Altec's own durability data (UV, oil, solvents, abrasion, service temperature) for PU3500 - in the TDS, not accessible.
- Whether Altec's UV laminate is an inline or manual step.
- Typical characters per tag/fonts used by installers - no source; inference only.

## 5. Comparable yellow cable-marker systems from other brands (industry pattern)

### Takeaway
The yellow, halogen-free, tie-wrap-mounted flat tag printed black by thermal transfer is an industry-wide pattern with near-identical formats: about 10-15 mm high x 40-100 mm long, about 0.5 mm thick, on rolls, ladders or perforated strips, with 60 x 10 mm as the common denominator. Geometry is similar across brands but feed details (slots, pitch, cartridges, chips) are brand-specific, so media is only loosely interchangeable.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- Design envelope for a pocket printer that complements the Altec workflow: flat rigid tags 10-15 mm high (optionally up to 25 mm), 40-100 mm long, about 0.4-0.6 mm thick, mass about 0.4-2.4 g per tag, polyurethane/polyolefin/polycarbonate, black resin thermal transfer at 200-300 dpi, print zone roughly 10 x 35-45 mm on the 60 x 10 mm format.
- Two mounting families exist: (a) direct tie-on tags with two slots (Altec ADC, Cembre ETF, Brady 7643, HT TIPTAG, Partex PPQZ) and (b) continuous yellow profile cut to length and slid into a carrier strip that is tie-wrapped to the cable (Partex PKH/POH + PRZ/PF, Cembre, Phoenix). Altec's ADC range is family (a).
- Because Altec's printer is a generic TSC roll printer and its tags are un-chipped roll stock, Altec media is in principle printable on any printer that can feed and register it - unlike Brady (chipped) or Phoenix/Cembre (cartridge/plate systems). That openness is what makes "print the same yellow tags on a pocket device" technically possible at all.
- Interchangeability across brands is geometric only (similar sizes); slot positions, pitch, registration marks and winding differ, so a pocket printer should either take Altec's tag web directly (cut-down strips/short rolls from the original roll) or define its own small cassette refilled with the same PU3500 stock.

## 6. Pain points of the current workflow

### Takeaway
No first-hand installer accounts (forums, reviews) about the Altec workflow were found; the "walking back and forth to the printer" pain point is supported only indirectly - by the hardware facts (3.75 kg mains-powered bench printer needing a PC/driver, label roll and 300 m ribbon) and by vendors marketing portable printers precisely "for on-site use without a fixed workstation".

### Cited Findings
- The ATP-300 Pro is a 3.75 kg, 226 x 198 x 332 mm desktop unit with USB/Ethernet (Wi-Fi/Bluetooth optional) — [ATP-300 Pro flyer](https://files.altec.nl/20240314091327/ATP-300Pro_-_A4-flyer.pdf)
- Printing from Altec's web designer requires a Windows printer driver plus the Altec Cloud Connector; cable lists are imported from Excel/CSV — [altec.nl PrintLabels.Online](https://altec.nl/en/software/product/printlabels-online/)
- Larger tag rolls (1000 per roll) need an external roll holder on the ATP-300/600 Pro — [altec.be THT HCM kabelmerker](https://www.altec.be/tht-60x10mm-hcm-kabelmerker-geel); [altec.nl ATP series](https://altec.nl/en/label-printers/atp-series/)
- Altec itself positions Brady M611/BMP61 portables for "small quantities of labels on location", implicitly acknowledging the desktop printer is not an on-site tool — [altec.nl blog on printer types](https://altec.nl/blog/de-verschillende-soorten-labelprinters/)
- Distributors market portable printers to installers and maintenance technicians "who need labels on site without a fixed workstation"; Brother markets battery P-touch units to electricians for cables and distribution boards — [Nedelko portable printers](https://www.nedelko.nl/identificatie/printers-and-software/portable-printers); [Brother NL electricians page](https://www.brother.nl/labels-en-bonnen/labelprinters-voor-electriciens)
- Phoenix Contact markets its mobile unit as "mobile printer for flexible use on site" — [Phoenix Contact mobile printers](https://www.phoenixcontact.com/en-pc/products/printers/mobile-printers)

### Inferences
- Workflow today: cable list prepared in Excel/DataPrint at the office or site hut, batch-printed on the ATP-300 Pro, tags torn off the roll and carried to the cable, fixed with two tie-wraps each. Any missing, wrong, damaged or newly added cable number requires a trip back to the printer and PC - the pain point named in the brief. This is an inference from the hardware/software setup, not a documented user complaint.
- Batch printing is efficient for planned panel builds (hundreds of tags from a list) but poor for corrections and ad-hoc additions in the field; a pocket printer is most valuable as a complement for the last 5-10% of tags rather than a replacement for bulk printing.
- Mounting time (two tie-wraps per tag) is a second pain point inherent to the media; WrapTags (self-fastening, 5-23 mm bundles) and ADC Single Fix exist to reduce it.
- Brother P-touch/Brady tape printers solve portability but print adhesive tapes or chipped proprietary tags, not the yellow tie-on tags specified in many Dutch industrial/infra projects - so they do not replace the Altec media.
