## 1. CE marking and other EU/NL product law: what applies, and does own-use equipment need CE?

### Takeaway
A BLE, Li-ion, USB-C-charged printer that is *supplied to anyone outside the company* (scenario B and C alike, whether paid or free) falls under the full stack: RED 2014/53/EU (safety, EMC, radio, and since 1 Aug 2025 cybersecurity where in scope), RoHS, REACH, WEEE and battery producer responsibility (both run by Stichting OPEN in NL), Battery Regulation 2023/1542 (end-user-replaceable battery from 18 Feb 2027), PPWR (from 12 Aug 2026), CRA (reporting since 11 Sep 2026, full regime 11 Dec 2027) and the new product-liability regime; there is **no volume threshold** that makes B lighter than C. For scenario A the Commission's RED Guide treats equipment "constructed for own use" as *not placed on the market*, so the DoC/CE formalities are not triggered by a market act, but national rules on putting into service/use of radio equipment and the employer's Arbowet duty to provide safe work equipment still apply.

### Cited Findings

**Radio Equipment Directive (RED) – core**
- The RED defines "placing on the market" as the first making available of radio equipment on the Union market and "putting into service" as the first use of radio equipment in the Union by its end-user (Art. 2). — [RED text, legislation.gov.uk copy](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2014/53/chapter/I)
- The Commission's RED Guide (several editions, including one dated October 2026) states that when radio equipment is constructed for own use, or bought by a consumer in a third country for personal use, "it is not considered to be placed on the market". — [RED Guide Oct 2026 (CIRCABC)](https://webgate.ec.europa.eu/circabc-ewpp/d/d/workspace/SpacesStore/d2d68489-33b8-46c2-a167-8c042daebbb3/download); [RED Guide 2018 copy](https://www.ibf-solutions.com/fileadmin/dateidownloads/radio-equipment-guide-2014-53-eu.pdf)
- The same Guide notes that Member States may have restrictions on putting into service of radio equipment (Art. 7 RED, Guide section 1.4); a secondary summary warns that own-use is "not a general self-build exemption" because national rules may still make RED requirements applicable. — [RED Guide Oct 2026 (CIRCABC)](https://webgate.ec.europa.eu/circabc-ewpp/d/d/workspace/SpacesStore/d2d68489-33b8-46c2-a167-8c042daebbb3/download); [IB-Lenhardt RED FAQ](https://ib-lenhardt.com/kb/faq/radio-equipment-directive-red)
- When a pre-certified radio module is built into a device there is no test report for the harmonised standards under Art. 3(2) and 3(3) for the *final* device; the final product must still be assessed against the RED. A certified module "saves time and cost compared to a chip-down design" but does not transfer its approval. — [VDE customer information RED](https://vde.com/resource/blob/2257746/305090fb24a3738e51dbe4a523c4be35/red-kundeninformation-en-data.pdf); [Microchip RED page](https://www.microchip.com/red)
- Standards normally listed for a BLE end product: EN 300 328 (radio), EN 301 489-1/-17 (EMC), EN 62368-1 (safety), EN 62311 or EN 62479 (RF exposure). — [Microchip RED page](https://www.microchip.com/red)
- Essential requirements and conformity routes: Art. 3(1)(a) safety (LVD objectives with no voltage limit), 3(1)(b) EMC, 3(2) efficient spectrum use, 3(3)(d)/(e)/(f) cybersecurity; Art. 17 allows internal production control (Annex II, i.e. self-declaration) for Art. 3(1), and for Art. 3(2)/3(3) only where harmonised standards are applied in full, otherwise a notified body (Annex III or IV); Art. 10 technical documentation and DoC kept 10 years; Art. 18-20 DoC and CE marking. **[pinpoint not re-verified]** — [Directive 2014/53/EU](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2014/53/oj)

**RED cybersecurity (Delegated Regulation 2022/30, EN 18031)**
- The EN 18031-1/-2/-3:2024 standards were cited in the Official Journal by Implementing Decision (EU) 2025/138 with restrictions; self-assessment is allowed only if the relevant EN 18031 standard is applied and the product is not affected by the restrictions. — [Spanish ministry guide on applying EN 18031](https://digital.gob.es/content/dam/portal-mtdfp/avance-digital/telecomunicacion-e-infraestructuras-digitales/areas_interes/equipos-telecomunicacion/documents/Guia-aplicacion-EN-18031.pdf); [SGS SafeGuardS 026/25](https://www.sgs.com/news/2025/02/safeguards-02625-eu-harmonizes-en-18031-standards); [Implementing Decision 2025/138](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dec_impl/2025/138/spa)
- One vendor source states that a product which lets users skip or bypass setting a password (EN 18031-1 clauses 6.2.5.1 and 6.2.5.2) loses the presumption of conformity and lands on the notified-body route. — [ByteSnap EN 18031 FAQ](https://www.bytesnap.com/news-blog/en-18031-compliance-cra-2026/)
- Scope of Art. 3(3)(d): Delegated Regulation 2022/30 covers radio equipment that can communicate over the internet "whether it communicates directly or via any other equipment". Orgalim's position paper says Bluetooth technology cannot be stated to be excluded: "depending on the communication protocol used", Bluetooth equipment "may or may not fall under the scope". A trade-press commentary notes that the case of a Bluetooth device connected to a smartphone "is not spelled out in the EN 18031-1 standard nor in the RED". — [Orgalim common understanding paper](https://orgalim.eu/wp-content/uploads/Common-understanding-of-the-term-Internet-connected-radio-equipment_130922-1.pdf); [In Compliance Magazine, March 2026](https://digital.incompliancemag.com/issue/march-2026/reds-cybersecurity-requirements-update-en-18031-x2024/)
- EnOcean has published its own assessment that the delegated act does not apply to its (non-IP) end products; this is one manufacturer's reading, not a regulator's. — [EnOcean statement](https://www.enocean.com/wp-content/uploads/support/Quality-and-Reliability/EnOcean-Statement-on-EN-18031-Standards-regarding-Cybersecurity-Requirements-for-Radio-Equipment.pdf)

**Common charger (USB-C)**
- Directive (EU) 2022/2380 added Art. 3(4) and Annex Ia to the RED. The listed categories are handheld mobile phones, tablets, digital cameras, headphones, headsets, handheld videogame consoles, portable speakers, e-readers, keyboards, mice, portable navigation systems and earbuds (from 28 Dec 2024) and laptops (from 28 Apr 2026). Label printers are not in the list. — [5M Global summary](https://5mglobal.com/?p=13974); [Directive (EU) 2022/2380](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2022/2380/oj/eng); [Commission common-charger page](https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/sectors/electrical-and-electronic-engineering-industries-eei/radio-equipment-directive-red/one-common-charging-solution-all_en)

**Battery Regulation (EU) 2023/1542**
- The Regulation entered into force 18 Aug 2023 and has applied since 18 Feb 2024. — [CMS, EU Sustainable Batteries Regulation: where are we now](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/eu-sustainable-batteries-regulation-where-are-we-now)
- From **18 February 2027** Art. 11 requires anyone placing on the market products incorporating portable batteries to ensure the batteries are readily removable and replaceable by the end-user. Commission guidelines under Art. 11(9) were published 10 Jan 2025 (C/2025/214). — [Commission guidelines C/2025/214](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/C/2025/214/oj/eng); [TÜV SÜD on Art. 11](https://www.tuvsud.com/en-gb/resource-centre/blogs/uk/testing-and-certification-blog/eu-battery-regulation-2027-article-11); [CDX regulatory news](https://public.cdxsystem.com/en/web/cdx/w/battery-regulation-new-guidelines-on-removability-requirements)
- Belonging to a product category does not automatically qualify for an exemption; wet-environment and medical derogations need evidence that user removal creates safety risks. — [CDX regulatory news](https://public.cdxsystem.com/en/web/cdx/w/battery-regulation-new-guidelines-on-removability-requirements)
- **New (Oct 2026):** Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/1770 of 14 July 2026, published in the OJ on 22 Oct 2026, replaces Art. 11(2) and widens the list of products whose portable battery may be replaceable *only by independent professionals*. Sources differ on the count (six added vs. eight in total); categories reported include wearables/smartwatches, professional medical imaging and radiotherapy equipment, in-vitro diagnostic devices, electric toys and ATEX equipment. A handheld label printer is not among the reported categories. One source gives entry into force as 11 Nov 2026; the 18 Feb 2027 application date of Art. 11 itself is unchanged. — [Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/1770](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_del/2026/1770/oj); [UL Solutions news](https://www.ul.com/news/eu-expands-portable-battery-removability-derogations); [Lovguiden summary 22 Oct 2026](https://www.lovguiden.dk/en/det-offentlige/eur-lex-eu-tidende/2026-10-22-seks-nye-undtagelser-fra-krav-om-udskiftelige-batterier-i-eu)
- Labelling: general battery label information applies from 18 Aug 2026 (or 18 months after the implementing act, if later); QR-code access to information from 18 Feb 2027. — [CMS](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/eu-sustainable-batteries-regulation-where-are-we-now); [Compliance Gate timeline](https://www.compliancegate.com/eu-batteries-regulation-timeline-and-dates/)
- Due diligence (Chapter VII): postponed from 18 Aug 2025 to **18 Aug 2027** by Regulation (EU) 2025/1561 (OJ 30 July 2025); Commission guidance deadline moved to 26 July 2026. — [NMI](https://nmi.nl/battery-due-diligence-deadline-postponed-but-the-clock-is-still-ticking/); [ESS News](https://www.ess-news.com/2025/07/22/eu-delays-battery-due-diligence-requirement)
- Due-diligence obligations do not apply to economic operators with net turnover below EUR 40 million (Art. 47); Art. 11 details: removable "with commercially available tools", no specialised/proprietary tools, heat or solvents; instructions and safety information to be supplied; battery available as spare part for at least 5 years after the last unit is placed on the market; software must not impede replacement; batteries carry their own CE marking and DoC (Art. 17-20) since 18 Aug 2024. **[pinpoint not re-verified; the search could not confirm the EUR 40 million figure or the 18 Aug 2024 CE date from secondary sources]** — [Regulation (EU) 2023/1542](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj)

**General Product Safety Regulation (EU) 2023/988**
- The GPSR general safety requirement targets products intended for consumers, plus products that under reasonably foreseeable conditions are likely to be used by consumers even if not intended for them (recital 9). Products exclusively for professional use and not reasonably likely to reach consumers are outside; there is no automatic B2B exemption, and professional equipment sold via a webshop open to private buyers may be caught. Labelling "for professional use only" and documenting the reasoning is advised; contractual anti-resale clauses alone are not considered enough. — [ProductIP: scope of the GPSR](https://www.productip.com/kb/productipedia/compliance-resources/scope-gpsr); [EUverify: B2B products and the GPSR](https://euverify.com/resource/b2b-products-and-the-eu-gpsr/); [Commission GPSR Q&A](https://webgate.ec.europa.eu/safety/consumers/consumers_safety_gate/obligationsForBusinesses/documents/Q&A.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Scenario A (own technicians only).** Based on the RED Guide wording, no placing on the market occurs, so the DoC, CE mark, technical file, Bluetooth-facing market obligations, CRA, GPSR, PPWR and most EPR registration duties are not triggered by a market act. What remains: (i) national rules on *use* of radio equipment and licence-exempt spectrum conditions (use an unmodified, pre-certified module with its approved antenna and power settings so the radio is demonstrably within EN 300 328 limits); (ii) Arbowet duties: suitable and safe work equipment, covered in the RI&E; the main real hazard is the Li-ion cell and charger; (iii) the Dutch sector statements that self-built equipment needs CE concern *machinery* (the Machinery Directive expressly covers manufacture for own use); a small battery printer is ordinary office/IT-type equipment under RED/LVD logic rather than a "machine", so that rule does not transfer one-to-one. This is an interpretation: a short written confirmation from RDI (radio) and an arbo-adviser is advisable.
- **The A/B boundary is fragile.** Handing units to subcontractors (zzp'ers), sister companies or partner firms, even on loan or free of charge, is "making available" and moves the project into scenario B. Many installation firms work with hired-in technicians, so scenario A only holds if the units stay with employees of the legal entity that built them.
- **Scenario B = scenario C in law.** None of RED, RoHS, the Battery Regulation, WEEE/AVV or PPWR has a small-series exemption. The fixed compliance cost is the same at 100 and at 10,000 units; only per-unit fees (OPEN, packaging) and insurance scale. Cost reduction must therefore come from design choices (pre-certified module and cell), self-declaration and lab choice, not from volume.
- **Cybersecurity scope is a design decision.** If the printer only talks to the phone over BLE and never relays internet traffic (no cloud account, firmware updates loaded from a local file by the app), there is a defensible argument that it is not "internet-connected radio equipment" and Art. 3(3)(d) does not apply; if the app relays cloud data or OTA firmware to the printer, treat it as in scope. Because the CRA will apply to every product with digital elements placed on the market from 11 Dec 2027 regardless, doing the EN 18031-1 self-assessment now (secure pairing, signed firmware updates, no default/blank credentials) is the low-regret choice. Document the scope analysis in the technical file either way.
- **USB-C is not legally mandated** for a label printer (not an Annex Ia category), but it is the obvious practical choice; if used, the common-charger labelling rules do not apply either.
- **Battery design for 2027.** Any unit placed on the market from 18 Feb 2027 needs a battery the user can swap with ordinary tools (screws, connector, no glue/solder), with replacement instructions and spare batteries available for 5 years after the last sale. A glued-in pouch cell is acceptable only for units placed on the market before that date. Design for a screw-closed compartment and a connectorised, protected pack from day one. If the company imports cells/packs from outside the EU it becomes the *importer* of the battery with its own Battery Regulation duties (check CE mark, DoC, labels); if it assembles packs itself it becomes the battery *manufacturer*. Buying a CE-marked, labelled pack from an EU supplier avoids both.
- **Laser:** thermal-transfer and thermal-inkjet printers contain no laser, so EN 60825-1 is irrelevant unless a laser-marking concept is chosen.

## 2. Test-lab costs and lead times in 2026; savings from pre-certified module and cell; EU vs Chinese labs; self-declaration vs notified body

### Takeaway
Published price indications for a simple BLE product built on a pre-certified module cluster around roughly EUR 3,000-10,000 for RF spot checks, EMC and safety at a commercial lab (Chinese labs quote from about USD 2,100-4,200; itemised Western figures add up to EUR 10,000-20,000 for a full, non-modular programme), with EN 18031 ranging from near-zero external cost (self-assessment) to EUR 15,000-40,000 (third-party lab). All of it can be self-declared without a notified body if harmonised standards are applied in full; none of the figures comes from a Dutch lab price list, so real quotes from Kiwa/Telefication, DEKRA or others are still needed.

- Itemised estimate (module vendor blog): RF testing EUR 4,000-8,000; EMC EUR 3,000-6,000; safety EN 62368-1 EUR 2,000-4,000; optional notified-body review EUR 1,000-2,000; total EUR 10,000-20,000 for RED. — [Tecksay compliance guide](https://www.tecksay.com/bluetooth-module-certification-compliance-a-practical-guide-en/)

(Attribution of the individual figures to the three vendor pages is from search summaries.) — [Lithium-ion pack certifications help article](https://intercom.help/lithium-ion/en/articles/1750688-learn-about-our-lithium-ion-pack-certifications); [Epec battery pack certifications](https://www.epectec.com/batteries/battery-pack-certifications.html); [BatterySpace IEC 62133 service](https://www.batteryspace.com/iec62133.aspx)
- For a pack to obtain IEC 62133, the cell must have passed IEC 62133 first. — [BatterySpace IEC 62133 service](https://www.batteryspace.com/iec62133.aspx)
- Self-declaration vs notified body: self-assessment is possible where the harmonised standards (including EN 18031 without triggering its restrictions) are applied. — [Spanish ministry guide on applying EN 18031](https://digital.gob.es/content/dam/portal-mtdfp/avance-digital/telecomunicacion-e-infraestructuras-digitales/areas_interes/equipos-telecomunicacion/documents/Guia-aplicacion-EN-18031.pdf)

### Inferences
- **What the pre-certified module saves.** The full conducted EN 300 328 programme (the EUR 4,000-8,000 RF line) shrinks to radiated spurious/power spot checks on the host, provided the module is used with its approved antenna, firmware power settings and layout guidance and the module maker supplies its RED test reports for the technical file. Rough saving EUR 3,000-6,000 and 1-3 weeks, plus far lower risk of an RF fail. It saves nothing on EMC, safety, EN 18031 or documentation. For FCC/ISED the saving is larger (no intentional-radiator certification at all).
- **What a pre-certified cell/pack saves.** Buying a protected cell or pack that already has UN 38.3 and IEC 62133-2 reports (and, from an EU supplier, its own CE/DoC under the Battery Regulation) avoids roughly USD 5,000-17,000 and 4-8 weeks of destructive testing and lets EN 62368-1 Annex M be satisfied largely by documentation. A custom pack (own BMS or cell combination) re-opens UN 38.3 and IEC 62133-2 at pack level.
- **Indicative budget for this product (researcher's synthesis of the ranges above, ex VAT, not lab quotes):**

- **Per scenario.** A: none of this is mandatory; a sensible minimum is a one-day pre-compliance EMC/radiated scan and a documented battery/charger safety review (order of EUR 500-2,000, estimate). B and C: the same fixed block applies, so at 100 units the typical CE subtotal alone is EUR 100-140 per unit and at 1,000 units EUR 10-14 per unit; adding Bluetooth SIG roughly doubles the fixed cost. For B, the cheapest defensible route is Chinese-lab test reports plus a self-written technical file and DoC, optionally reviewed by a Dutch consultant; for C, EU-lab reports (Kiwa/Telefication, DEKRA, TÜV, Eurofins) are easier to defend to RDI, distributors and insurers.
- **Notified body:** not needed if EN 300 328, EN 301 489, EN 62368-1 and EN 18031-1 are applied in full and the EN 18031 restrictions are avoided by design (mandatory authentication, no skip-password option). A voluntary NB type-examination (EUR 1,000-5,000 in the cited ranges) only buys comfort.
- **Design choices that cut test cost:** no mains part (5 V USB input only, charger not supplied), certified module with integral antenna, certified protected cell, metal-free plastic enclosure with adequate ESD paths, and freezing hardware before testing (re-tests after layout or antenna changes are the usual budget killer).

## 3. Other markets: UKCA, FCC/ISED, Bluetooth SIG, USB-IF

### Takeaway

### Inferences
- For scenarios B and C the Bluetooth SIG fee (about EUR 10,000-11,000 at the Adopter rate, or about EUR 7,000 plus whatever the Contributing Adopter dues are) is likely the single largest compliance item, larger than the whole CE test programme at a budget lab. ## 4. Companion app: Apple/Google requirements and costs, cross-platform options, Web Bluetooth, effort, installer functionality

### Takeaway
Publishing is cheap in fees but has process hurdles: Google requires new *personal* developer accounts to run a 14-day closed test with at least 12 testers before production (organisation accounts need a D-U-N-S number instead), and Apple review expects the hardware or a convincing demo; Web Bluetooth remains unusable in iOS Safari, so a no-app web tool can only serve Android/desktop Chrome unless users install a niche Web-BLE browser. ### Cited Findings

## 5. Reusing existing ecosystems: reverse-engineered protocols, open-source libraries, vendor SDKs, and the legal position

### Takeaway
Mature MIT/GPL-licensed open-source implementations exist for Niimbot, Phomemo and Brother P-touch protocols, so an own app could drive an existing low-cost printer in scenario A/B without building hardware; EU software law protects observing and testing a lawfully obtained program and denies copyright in functionality and data formats, but decompiling code is only allowed under the narrow interoperability conditions of Art. 6, and consumable-authentication (RFID) schemes add contractual and practical risk rather than a clear statutory bar.

### Cited Findings
- NiimBlueLib (MultiMote): MIT licence; TypeScript/JavaScript library for browser (Web Bluetooth) and Node; describes itself as the most accurate open-source implementation of the Niimbot protocol; disclaimer "intended for informational and educational purposes only", "not affiliated with or endorsed by the original software or hardware vendor". — [NiimBlueLib (GitHub)](https://github.com/MultiMote/niimbluelib)
- Niimbot protocol community documentation: most printers use BLE characteristic bef8d6c9-9c21-4c9e-b632-bd58c1009f9f of service e7810a71-73ae-499d-8c15-faa9aef0c3f2; each label roll contains an RFID tag identifying label type and remaining print count; label size cannot be derived from the tag without a request to print.niimbot.com. — [printers.niim.blue: connecting](https://printers.niim.blue/interfacing/connecting/); [printers.niim.blue: label types](https://printers.niim.blue/other/label-types/)
- niimprint (AndBondStyle fork of kjy00302): MIT; Python 3.11; USB-serial and Bluetooth; tested on B1, B18, B21, D11, D110; about 203 dpi; max width 384 px (B-series) or 96 px (D11); does not verify label size. — [niimprint (GitHub)](https://github.com/AndBondStyle/niimprint)
- Other Niimbot implementations: a Flutter package `niim_blue_flutter`, a Home Assistant integration exposing RFID/label-remaining sensors, an Android port. — [niim_blue_flutter (pub.dev)](https://pub.dev/packages/niim_blue_flutter/install); [hass-niimbot (GitHub)](https://github.com/eigger/hass-niimbot); [android_niimprint (GitHub)](https://www.github.com/terratempest/android_niimprint)
- User reviews report that third-party labels print poorly on a Niimbot unless an RFID sticker from an original roll is attached (anecdotal). — [Trustpilot reviews](https://se.trustpilot.com/review/niimbotlabel.com)
- phomemo-tools: GPL-3.0; Python + CUPS; supports M02/M02 Pro/M02S, M110/M120/M220, T02, M832; protocol obtained by sniffing Bluetooth packets of the Android app and dumping USB packets. — [phomemo-tools (GitHub)](https://github.com/vivier/phomemo-tools)
- ptouch-print: command-line tool for Brother P-touch over libusb (no CUPS); GPL-3.0 fork listed; models include PT-2430PC, PT-P700, PT-D450; upstream by Dominic Radermacher. — [ptouch-print fork (GitHub)](https://github.com/patrislav1/ptouch-print); [ecosyste.ms listing](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Fpatrislav1%2Fptouch-print)
- CJEU C-406/10 SAS Institute v World Programming (2 May 2012): neither the functionality of a program nor its programming language or data file formats are protected by software copyright; a lawful user may observe, study and test the program to determine its underlying ideas (Art. 5(3)), and contract terms to the contrary are void (Art. 9(1), now Art. 8); WPL had no source-code access and did not decompile. Copying source code or decompiling object code to recreate functionality could still infringe. — [Kluwer Copyright Blog](https://legalblogs.wolterskluwer.com/copyright-blog/decrypting-the-code-cjeu-sas-vs-world-programming/); [CMS: SAS v WPL](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/sas-v-wpl-who-dares-loses); [Mondaq summary](https://www.mondaq.com/copyright/182142/software-functionality-or-language-not-protected-says-ecj)
- Directive 2009/24/EC Art. 6 permits decompilation without the rightholder's authorisation only where indispensable to obtain information needed for interoperability of an independently created program, by a licensee/lawful user, where that information is not already readily available, confined to the necessary parts; the information may not be used for other goals, passed on unnecessarily, or used to create a substantially similar program. Art. 5(3) covers black-box observation; Art. 8 voids contrary contract terms. **[pinpoint not re-verified]** — [Directive 2009/24/EC](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2009/24/oj)

### Inferences
- **Lowest-risk technical route:** sniffing BLE traffic between a purchased printer and its official app, and writing an independent implementation of the observed protocol, is black-box analysis (Art. 5(3) logic, SAS v WPL) and does not copy protected expression. Decompiling the vendor's Android app goes further and is only covered if the Art. 6 conditions are met; reusing the open-source libraries shifts, but does not remove, that question (their authors' methods are not always documented). The libraries' MIT/GPL licences govern the library code only; GPL-3.0 code (phomemo-tools, ptouch-print) cannot be linked into a closed-source app.
- **Business risk rather than legal risk:** a product or service that depends on a third-party printer's undocumented protocol can be broken by a firmware update, by cloud checks (Niimbot label metadata requires a call to the vendor's server) or by RFID-locked consumables. This is acceptable for scenario A (internal tooling, quick start) and as a prototype path for B, but weak as the foundation of a commercial offer in C. Selling an app "for Niimbot printers" also needs care with trademark use (see section 6).
- **Vendor SDKs are the cleaner route for B:** Brother, Brady, Zebra and several Chinese OEMs (HPRT/Hanin, Supvan, Niimbot for partners) publish mobile SDKs; an own-brand app on top of an OEM printer with an official SDK or an OEM/ODM agreement avoids both the reverse-engineering question and most of the certification cost, because the OEM remains the manufacturer (or the Dutch company becomes importer/own-brand "manufacturer" relying on the OEM's test reports).
- **Circumventing consumable authentication** (cloning RFID tags) is legally distinct from protocol interoperability and should not be assumed safe: it may engage technical-protection-measure rules, contract terms and, for cloned tags carrying the vendor's data, other IP. Needs legal advice before commercial use.

### Gaps
- Searches for the official SDK terms (Brother Print SDK/Mobile SDK, Brady SDK, Zebra Link-OS, HPRT/Hanin, Supvan, Niimbot partner SDK) could not be run (search budget exhausted); licence conditions, cost and supported models are unverified.
- The Dutch implementation (Auteurswet art. 45j-45n) and CJEU C-13/20 Top System (decompilation for error correction) were not retrieved.
- No case law was found on RFID-authenticated label consumables specifically; the legal position on compatible consumables with authentication chips in the EU needs professional advice.
- I did not verify whether the Niimbot/Phomemo/Supvan app licence terms prohibit reverse engineering (such clauses are void for Art. 5(3)/6 acts within the EU, but may matter elsewhere).

## 6. Patents and IP: landscape, expired fundamentals, FTO and protection costs, printing on a third party's (Altec's) labels

### Takeaway
I found evidence that Brother actively enforces tape-cassette patents and that Niimbot (Wuhan Jingchen) holds recent utility and design patents on label printers with separated consumables, but no reliable patent-family mapping for the other named companies could be produced in this session; a proper FTO search by a Dutch patent attorney is needed before scenario B/C. Printing on genuine, lawfully purchased third-party labels and naming them descriptively ("suitable for ... labels") is in principle permitted under EU trade-mark law if done honestly and without the owner's logo, while own protection is cheap for designs and trade marks (EUR 350 and EUR 850 official fees) and expensive for patents (EUR 5,000-10,000 for a Dutch filing with attorney; about EUR 5,600 in EPO official fees alone for a European patent).

### Cited Findings
- Brother enforcement: in 2013 Brother sued several suppliers of compatible P-touch tape cassettes in the District of Delaware on a design patent and utility patents; it obtained an injunction against Aster Graphics, a consent judgment against EZ Replacement Labels (three US patents covering tape cassettes), and settlements with Aster Graphics, LD Products and The Supplies Guys. — [PR Newswire: Brother enforces P-touch tape cassette patents](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/brother-enforces-p-touch-tape-cassette-patents-216735411.html); [The Recycler: injunction against Aster](https://archive.therecycler.com/?p=573060); [Actionable Intelligence: Brother resolves complaint](https://www.action-intell.com/?p=13446); [RT Media: Brother settles with Aster](https://www.rtmworld.com/news/brother-settles-with-aster-graphics/)

### Inferences
- **A pocket printer that prints on genuine Altec labels:** (interpretation, needs confirmation by an IP lawyer)
  - *Using purchased consumables:* once genuine labels have been sold in the EEA with the right-holder's consent, the buyer may use them as he wishes; rights in the labels themselves (patent, design, trade mark) are exhausted for those items. Designing a device that accepts them is not in itself an infringement of rights in the labels.
  - *Patent risk sits elsewhere:* check whether Altec (or its supplier) holds patents claiming a *printer*, a *printing method* or a *system of printer plus label carrier*. Such claims are not exhausted by the sale of labels and could be infringed by the new device. This is the first question for the FTO search, together with general handheld-printer mechanics (single-label feed, sensing, cutter).
  - *Trade mark:* statements such as "geschikt voor Altec [type] kabellabels" are covered by Art. 14(1)(c) EUTMR (and the equivalent Benelux rule) if they are factual, use the word mark only as much as needed, avoid Altec's logo/get-up, and do not suggest a commercial link or disparage. Do not put the Altec name on the device itself.
  - *Design rights in the label shape* matter only if the company makes or sells look-alike labels; a printer path dimensioned to fit the label is functional interface geometry. Selling own-brand copies of the yellow labels would raise separate design, trade-mark (colour/shape marks) and slavish-imitation questions.
  - *Commercial reality:* dependence on a single third-party consumable is a supply and pricing risk; a cooperation or licence with Altec would remove most legal uncertainty and could be a route to market.
- **Expired fundamentals:** given the 20-year maximum patent term, anything filed before late 2006 (basic thermal-transfer printing, ribbon-and-tape cassettes of the original Brother TZ and Dymo D1 generations, gap/black-mark label sensing) is free to use; risk concentrates in post-2007 improvement patents: cassette identification and RFID/NFC consumable authentication, half-cut/full-cut mechanisms, "separated consumables" architectures (Niimbot US 12,179,478), smartphone-app-controlled printing workflows, and registered designs on housings and cassettes. A design-around strategy (no proprietary cassette, plain label feed, own industrial design) reduces exposure.
- **FTO cost (researcher's estimate, no Dutch tariff source found):** a focused FTO quick-scan by a Dutch patent attorney on 2-3 core features is likely in the range EUR 2,500-6,000; a full FTO with written opinion across the named competitors EUR 8,000-20,000 (the VLAIO EUR 20,000 cap fits the upper end). A free orientation session at Octrooicentrum Nederland (RVO) and own Espacenet searches can narrow the scope first.
- **Own protection, staged by scenario:** A: nothing needed beyond keeping the design confidential. B: EU design registration for the housing (EUR 350 official) and an EU trade mark (EUR 850) are the cost-effective basics; total with an agent roughly EUR 1,500-3,000. C: consider a Dutch priority filing (EUR 5,000-10,000 with attorney; note the coming substantive examination), followed within 12 months by a decision on a European/unitary patent (official fees about EUR 5,600 before attorney, translation and renewal costs; realistic all-in EUR 15,000-30,000 over the grant phase, estimate). A patent only makes sense if there is a genuinely new technical feature (for example the single-label feed for pre-cut cable markers); the idea "pocket printer for existing labels" as such is unlikely to be patentable.
- A Dutch patent also unlocks the Innovatiebox route less than the WBSO does (see section 7); it is not required for it.

## 7. Dutch subsidies and tax schemes for SME product development (2026, with 2027 changes)

### Takeaway

### Inferences

## 8. Product liability, warranty and insurance for a Dutch producer

### Takeaway
The new Product Liability Directive (EU) 2024/2853 must be transposed by 9 December 2026; the Netherlands is amending Books 6 and 7 of the Civil Code by "pure" transposition, keeps the development-risk defence, and the regime now explicitly covers software and removes the old claim thresholds. No Dutch premium figures for a small electronics producer were found; general benchmarks point to a few hundred to roughly EUR 1,500 per year.

### Cited Findings
- Directive (EU) 2024/2853 entered into force on 8 Dec 2024; Member States must transpose by **9 Dec 2026**. — [Hogan Lovells: implementing the new PLD in the Netherlands (10 Nov 2026)](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/implementing-the-new-eu-product-liability-directive-in-the-netherlands)
- "Product" now includes software (including AI systems); software updates and post-sale conduct can bear on defectiveness. — [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/implementing-the-new-eu-product-liability-directive-in-the-netherlands); [Global Law Experts (secondary)](https://globallawexperts.com/eu-product-liability-netherlands/)
- Dutch approach: amendment of Books 6 and 7 BW and the transitional act, no stand-alone statute, no gold-plating; the Netherlands retains the development-risk defence. Final enactment could not be confirmed from the results (an October 2026 tracker exists). — [CMS guide: Netherlands](https://cms.law/en/int/expert-guides/CMS-Guide-to-changes-to-the-EU-Product-Liability-Directive/Netherlands); [CMS transposition update](https://cms.law/en/nld/legal-updates/transposition-time-netherlands-finland-and-sweden-update-on-the-eu-member-states-adoption-of-the-new-product-liability-directive); [Faegre Drinker progress report, Oct 2026](https://www.faegredrinker.com/zh/insights/publications/2026/10/transposing-the-eu-new-product-liability-directive-a-member-state-progress-report-october-2026-update)
- The new directive removes the minimum and maximum claim thresholds of the 1985 regime. — [Mondaq/Buren](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/product-liability-safety/1594076/the-new-product-liability-directive-what-do-you-need-to-know); [Stibbe](https://www.stibbe.com/nl/publications-and-insights/nieuwe-eu-regels-inzake-aansprakelijkheid-voor-gebrekkige-producten)
- The new rules apply to products placed on the market or put into service after 9 Dec 2026 (older products stay under Directive 85/374/EEC); liability is strict, towards natural persons, for death/personal injury, damage to property not used exclusively for professional purposes, and loss of non-professional data; it cannot be excluded by contract; limitation 3 years, long-stop 10 years (25 for latent personal injury); courts can order disclosure of evidence and presume defect/causation in defined cases. **[pinpoint not re-verified]** — [Directive (EU) 2024/2853](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2024/2853/oj)
- A producer is liable for defects in its product, and so is a brand owner who puts its name on a product (Dutch insurance adviser's white paper). — [Zicht white paper aansprakelijkheid](https://www.zichtadviseurs.nl/app/uploads/2022/11/Zicht-Whitepaper-Aansprakelijkheid_2211.pdf)
- Premium benchmarks: US industry data put average product-liability premiums for manufacturers/wholesalers/retailers under USD 1 million revenue at about USD 1,192 per year (USD 1M/2M limits), low-risk products a few hundred dollars, high-risk several times the average; another US average is about USD 500 per year. Dutch general-liability (AVB) examples: about EUR 37 per month for an agency with EUR 1.5 million revenue and EUR 2.5 million cover, about EUR 24 per month for a clothing shop with EUR 750,000 revenue. None is specific to Dutch electronics producers. — [Beancount guide, Sept 2026](https://beancount.io/nl/blog/2026/09/16/product-liability-insurance-manufacturers-makers-resellers-guide); [Shopify NL](https://www.shopify.com/nl/blog/productaansprakelijkheidsverzekering); [Ondernemen met personeel](https://ondernemenmetpersoneel.nl/mijn-branche/detailhandel/bedrijfsaansprakelijkheid-de-detailhandel); [KvK on business insurance](https://www.kvk.nl/starten/betalen-bij-schade-met-deze-verzekeringen-voorkom-je-dat/)

### Inferences
- **Who can claim what.** Because strict product liability protects natural persons and excludes property used exclusively for professional purposes, the realistic PLD exposure of a professional cable-label printer is personal injury (Li-ion fire or burn) and damage to a technician's private property; a wrongly labelled cable causing business loss at a customer is a contract/tort matter, which *can* be limited in B2B terms. The device contains a lithium cell and is carried in pockets, so the injury scenario, though low-probability, justifies insurance in B and C.
- **Scenario A:** liability runs through employer's liability (art. 7:658 BW) and the RI&E; the existing company AVB normally covers injury to employees and third parties, but the insurer should be told the company builds its own battery-powered tools. Note the PLD also applies to products "put into service", so own-use does not place the company wholly outside the regime towards third parties.
- **Scenario B/C:** the Dutch company is "manufacturer" (also if it only brands an OEM product, and "importer" if it imports from outside the EU). It needs: product liability cover (in Dutch AVB policies usually a separate product-liability section that must be explicitly included, with attention to recall costs and worldwide/US exclusions), B2B general terms limiting liability and setting a 12- or 24-month commercial warranty, traceability (serial numbers, batch records for cells), and a technical file that doubles as its defence. The new evidence-disclosure and presumption rules make a well-kept technical file and test reports materially more valuable.
- **Software as product:** the companion app and firmware are "products"; a defect introduced by an update, or failing to supply a needed security update, can create liability after sale. This aligns with the CRA support-period duty.
- **Insurance cost indication (estimate, not a quote):** for a small producer with turnover under EUR 500,000 and EUR 1.25-2.5 million cover, expect roughly EUR 500-1,500 per year for AVB including product liability in the EU; battery-powered products, recall cover and non-EU sales push it higher. Obtain quotes via a broker once a CE file and certified-cell evidence exist; insurers ask for them.
- **Warranty:** for consumer buyers Dutch law requires conformity for the product's reasonably expected life (not a fixed two years), with the EU-wide two-year minimum and one-year reversed burden of proof as floor; B2B sales allow contractual warranty terms. Since installers buy as businesses, clear B2B terms matter more than consumer rules, but sole traders buying via a public webshop may be treated like consumers in some respects. This needs to be confirmed by a lawyer when drafting the terms.

- Dutch consumer-sale and B2B warranty rules (BW 7:17, 7:18, 7:23, 6:233 ff.) were not sourced in this session; the warranty inference is unverified background and should be checked.
- No Dutch insurer or broker premium data for a small electronics/battery-product manufacturer was found; whether standard Dutch AVB policies include or exclude product liability varies by policy (one search summary claimed standard AVBs exclude it, which conflicts with common Dutch practice of a separate product-liability section; verify with a broker).
- Recall-cost insurance and cyber-liability cover for the app were not researched.
