## 1. Thermal transfer in miniature: print heads, existing small devices, teardowns

### Takeaway
A 12 mm / 96-dot / 203 dpi head is the de-facto standard in pocket label printers (Niimbot D11/B18, AiYin D11s), and the smallest commercial thermal-transfer (ribbon) device found, the Niimbot B18, is 137 x 90 x 37 mm / 310 g, i.e. roughly 3x the target volume. No off-the-shelf catalogue head from Kyocera/Rohm narrower than about 40 mm with public dimensions, energy and price was found; the 12 mm heads are effectively only obtainable as spare parts or via Chinese OEM channels, so head sourcing data is the biggest documentation gap.

### Cited Findings
- Working principle / energy definition: ROHM defines "applied energy" in a datasheet as the energy consumed by the heat elements only; the printer must supply more to cover drive-circuit losses, and the maximum applied-energy curve in the datasheet determines head life and must never be exceeded — [ROHM TechWeb](https://techweb.rohm.com/product/printhead/printheads/23770/)
- Example energy level: a ROHM 24 V receipt-printer head lists a standard operating point of 0.215 mJ/dot at a scanning-line time of 0.82 ms (part attribution from a search excerpt, probably KD2004-DAGW00A; verify in the PDF) — [ROHM datasheet](https://fscdn.rohm.com/en/products/databook/datasheet/module/printhead/kd2004-dagw00a-e.pdf)

### Inferences
- Per-dot electrical model (from the Seiko numbers above): I_dot = Vp / R. With R about 166 ohm: 57 mA/dot at 9.5 V (64 dots x 57 mA = 3.66 A, which matches the cited 3.66 A figure, so the model is consistent), about 45 mA and 0.33 W per dot at 7.4 V, about 22 mA and 0.08 W per dot at 3.7 V. Energy per dot = V^2 / R x t_on, so 0.2-0.3 mJ/dot needs about 0.6-0.9 ms at 7.4 V but about 2.4-3.6 ms at 3.7 V.
- For a 96-dot head, "all dots on" would draw about 4.3 A at 7.4 V (32 W) with 166 ohm elements. Small printers avoid this by splitting each line into several strobe groups and by limiting print speed; the Brady M211's 15 mm/s and Brother's 20 mm/s are consistent with energy-limited battery designs.
- Thermal transfer with resin ribbon generally needs more head energy than direct thermal paper (hotter, slower). Assume 0.3-0.5 mJ/dot for resin on plastic as a planning number until measured.
- Size gap: B18 envelope is 137 x 90 x 37 = 456 cm3, Brady M211 about 920 cm3, versus a 100-200 cm3 target. Both carry a label roll/cassette inside; removing the media roll (single-tag feed) is what makes a 2-4x reduction conceivable.
- None of the mini devices' main SoCs were identified by a source. The LuckPrinter white-label SDK (see section 9) and the JL chip in cat printers suggest the Chinese devices use low-cost Bluetooth-audio-class SoCs (Jieli or similar) rather than ESP32/nRF52.

### Gaps
- No public datasheet (dimensions, voltage, resistance, energy, price) was found for a 12-15 mm, 96-dot or 24 mm, 192-dot 203/300 dpi head from Kyocera, Rohm, SHEC (Shandong Hualing), AOI, Gaoqi, Alps or Toshiba Hokuto. These parts must be requested from the vendors or bought as D11/B18/P-touch spare heads and measured. Head price at low volume is unknown.
- No teardown of the Niimbot B18, D11/D110, or Brady M211 was found (motor type, gear train, SoC, ribbon clutch all unconfirmed). FCC internal photos were not reachable in this session. Buying and opening a B18 (about USD 100) is the fastest way to close this gap.
- Brother PT-E310BT battery capacity and internals were not found. TZe cassette shell dimensions and internal layout were not found in a primary source.
- Kyocera announced a "world's smallest thermal printhead for barcode printing" in 2016, but only the headline was retrieved — [Kyocera news](https://europe.kyocera.com/index/news/previous_news/news_archive_detail.L3RoZXJtYWxfcHJpbnRoZWFkcy9uZXdzLzIwMTYvS1lPQ0VSQV9EZXZlbG9wc19Xb3JsZF9zX1NtYWxsZXN0X1RoZXJtYWxfUHJpbnRoZWFkX2Zvcl9CYXJjb2RlX1ByaW50aW5n.html)

## 2. Ribbon handling: cassette size, resin ribbon on PVC/polyolefin, flat-head vs near-edge, reuse of commercial ribbons

### Takeaway
Ribbon itself is thin (about 6-9 micrometres), so 500-1000 tags of 40 mm need only about 20-45 m and two spools of roughly 17-25 mm diameter; ribbon volume is not the bottleneck, the take-up drive/clutch and the cassette width (ribbon width plus flanges) are. Resin or wax-resin ribbon on a flat-head geometry is the conservative choice; slitting standard 110 mm resin ribbon to 13-15 mm or reusing the Niimbot B18 16 m cassette are both plausible but unverified sources.

### Cited Findings
- Resin ribbon construction: Zebra 4400 resin has a 4.5 micrometre PET base and 8.8 micrometre total caliper (+/-10 %); DNP TR4500 (near-edge) is 4.5 + 3.4 = 7.9 micrometre; Pacific Barcode V300 is 5.8 +/- 0.8 micrometre total; Videojet resin is 4.5 micrometre PET with total below 9 micrometre — [Zebra 4400 resin](https://www.zebra.com/gb/en/products/spec-sheets/supplies/ribbons/4400-resin.html), [DNP TR4500](https://www.global.dnp/content/dam/dnp-global/biz/ima/solution/%E3%82%B3%E3%83%BC%E3%83%87%E3%82%A3%E3%83%B3%E3%82%B0_%E3%82%AB%E3%82%BF%E3%83%AD%E3%82%B0/TR4500_en_2026_05.pdf), [V300 datasheet](https://pacificbarcode.com/TTR/V300-datasheet.pdf), [Videojet resin ribbon](https://global.videojet.com/wp-content/uploads/dam/pdf/UK%20-%20English/Specification-Sheet/ss-ribbon-resin-en.pdf)
- Standard industrial ribbons come on 25.4 mm (1 inch) cores in 110 mm x 300 m rolls (example with 7.0 micrometre total thickness) — [RS datasheet](https://docs.rs-online.com/3a97/A700000013862992.pdf)
- Flat-head printers have a fixed head and can use wax, wax/resin or resin ribbons; near-edge printers use a floating head angled up to 45 degrees, run faster, and are limited to wax/resin or resin near-edge formulations — [Ricoh: flat head vs near edge](https://www.rei.ricoh.com/newsroom-blog/flat-head-vs-near-edge-printer/), [Avery Dennison technical bulletin](https://label.averydennison.com/content/dam/averydennison/lpm-responsive/na/doc/technical-bulletins/technical%20marketing%20bulletins/thermal-transfer-printers-technical-bulletin.pdf)
- Ricoh describes wax-resin ribbon as suitable for paper, PVC and synthetic materials with high abrasion and temperature resistance, and sells specialty resins with resistance to oils, moisture and cold-storage conditions — [Ricoh wax-resin](https://www.ricoh.co.th/en/insights/2025/08/28/wax-resin), [Ricoh ribbon types](https://www.rei.ricoh.com/newsroom-blog/understanding-resin-wax-resin-and-wax-ribbons/)
- General troubleshooting rule: on smudging or poor adhesion first check ribbon/media compatibility, then raise head temperature or lower speed — [Ricoh TTR guide](https://www.rei.ricoh.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/PS-1654-TTR-Guide.pdf)
- Niimbot B18 ribbon cassettes: "up to 16 m" per cassette, sold in black, white, red, blue, green; priced about EUR 6.35-9.95 or AUD 17-18; one listing instead states 160 cm (conflict, probably a typo); listings mention anti-counterfeiting chips for consumable identification; a seller claims water/oil/temperature resistance and 12-year durability (unverified seller claim) — [JB Hi-Fi](https://www.jbhifi.com.au/products/niimbot-compatible-labeling-ribbon-16m-for-b18-red), [Arvutitark](https://arvutitark.ee/en/accessories/consumables/ribbon-for-b18-niimbot-black-1397122), [iotball](https://iotball.com/en/products/accessories/printing/label-printers/black-thermal-transfer-ribbon-for-niimbot-printer-16197.html)
- Brother TZe cassettes carry 8 m of tape in widths 3.5-24 mm; laminated and non-laminated variants exist — [Brother TZe-335](https://store.brother.ee/catalogs/brotherestonia/supplies/ptouch/tapes/tze/tze335), [Icecat TZe](https://icecat.co.uk/us/p/brother/tze-335/label-making+tapes-4977766685733-tze-335-8960977.html)
- In the Brother PT-9200PC the same stepper advances tape and ink ribbon together (1:1 ribbon consumption) — [PT-9200PC service manual](https://archive.org/stream/printermanual-brother-pt-9200pc-service-manual/brotherpt-9200pcservicemanual_djvu.txt)

### Inferences
- Spool-size arithmetic (wound roll: D^2 = d_core^2 + 4 x L x t / pi, using t = 10 micrometre effective to allow for winding slack, core 8 mm):
  - 16 m (B18 cassette, about 350 tags of 40 mm + 5 mm gap): D about 16.4 mm
  - 22 m (about 500 tags): D about 18.5 mm
  - 45 m (about 1000 tags): D about 25 mm
  - Supply plus take-up side by side for 1000 tags: about 50 x 25 x 16 mm = 20 cm3 gross; for 350-500 tags about 35-38 x 18 x 16 mm = 10-11 cm3. A co-axial or overlapping-spool layout (take-up grows as supply shrinks, as in audio cassettes and TZe) saves about 30 %.
- Ribbon is consumed 1:1 with tag length while the head is down. Ribbon saving (lifting the head over blank areas) needs a head-lift actuator and is not realistic in this envelope; accept 1:1.
- Ribbon width should exceed print width by 1-2 mm per side: 13-15 mm ribbon for a 12 mm head. With cassette flanges and walls this gives a cassette thickness of about 17-19 mm; this, not the spool diameter, sets one cross-section dimension of the device.
- Take-up needs a slip clutch (felt/spring friction or magnetic) geared slightly faster than the platen surface speed, and the supply needs light back-tension to prevent wrinkling. Torque values were not found in sources; with a 12-15 mm wide, 6-9 micrometre ribbon the tension must be low (order of 0.1-0.3 N) to avoid stretching or tearing. Validate experimentally.
- Reuse options, ranked by practicality: (1) B18 16 m cassette as-is (right width, includes spools, but chip-locked to Niimbot firmware; in an own design the chip can simply be ignored); (2) 110 mm resin ribbon slit to 13-15 mm and rewound on own spools (cheapest per metre and free choice of resin grade matched to PVC/PO, but needs a slitting/rewinding jig); (3) TZe cassettes are not suitable: the ribbon is integrated with the tape in one cassette and laminated TZe prints mirror-image onto the laminate.
- Near-edge/true-edge heads give a straighter media path (good for rigid tags) and peel the ribbon immediately after the heater, but they need near-edge ribbon grades and a different head mount. A flat head with a small, soft platen roller is the default in every pocket device found.

### Gaps
- No source gave ribbon take-up torque/clutch design values, or minimum spool core diameter for resin ribbon.
- No source tested Niimbot B18 ribbon (or any specific ribbon grade) on rigid yellow PVC or halogen-free polyolefin cable markers. Adhesion, rub and solvent resistance must be tested per tag material; halogen-free polyolefin has low surface energy and is the higher-risk substrate.
- Whether B18 ribbon is wax-resin or full resin is not stated anywhere reliable.

## 3. Feeding single rigid tags: mechanisms, registration, head pressure, why rigid plastic is harder

### Takeaway
Industrial printers for rigid markers (Cembre MG3, Phoenix Contact THERMOMARK CARD) do not pinch-feed loose tags; they print tags held in plates, cards or magazines on 7-10 kg desktop machines, which signals that fixturing a rigid part under the head is the hard part. For a pocket device the realistic options are a pinch-fed straight-through path with a compliant platen, or a small shuttle/sled; there is no published head-pressure value, so this is the main item needing physical prototyping.

### Cited Findings
- Cembre MG3: thermal transfer at 300 dpi class resolution (300 x 600 dpi per UK listing), print area 110 x 140 mm, markers are printed on plates ("thousands of tags using just three plates" for cables, pipes, tubes); 382 x 230 x 221 mm and 9.6 kg per one distributor (another data sheet suggests about 7.4 kg); the MG3 is obsolete and superseded by the MG4 — [Cembre MG3](https://products.cembre.com/en/row/product/MG3), [Hilltop Products](https://hilltop-products.co.uk/cable-markers-identification/label-printers/thermal-transfer-label-printer-systems/cembre-printers-and-accessories/cembre-mg3-markingenius-printer.html), [emakgroup](https://emakgroup.kz/en/products/p73216262-termotransfernyj-printer-markinggenius)
- Phoenix Contact THERMOMARK CARD 2.0: 300 dpi, 104 mm print width, 254 x 194 x 325 mm, about 9.9 kg; card-format markers are loaded through format-specific magazines of about 114 x 11.5 x 166 mm — [Phoenix Contact 1085267 datasheet](https://www.phoenixcontact.com/us/products/1085267/pdf), [SOS electronic copy](https://cdn.soselectronic.com/productdata/04/c2/41a87250/thermomark-card-2-0-1085267.pdf)
- Partex Promark T-1000 is described as a professional, portable thermal-transfer printer for markers, fed from profiles on compact reels (PP+, PPA+, PO-068), i.e. continuous profile rather than loose single tags — [Partex catalogue](https://apsindustrial.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Partex-Catalogue.pdf)
- Tag material/thickness data points: Partex PPQ+ is 1.0 mm thick PVC; the halogen-free PPQZ is 0.6 mm thick "Zerex"; Partex's halogen-free range is rated -40 to +90 C; PP+ flat profiles are 6.3, 10.0 and 11.0 mm high — [TML Partex tags](https://tml.lt/en/product-category/wire-n-cable-marking/thermal-transfer-printers-en/cable-tags-ppq/tags-partex-sweden/), [Partex brochure 2024](https://sm-strojkoplast.si/uploads/2024/03/Partex-brosura-2024.pdf)
- Phoenix Contact sells halogen-free cable markers in 55 x 15 mm and 55 x 25 mm formats (WMTB HF), on rolls for thermal transfer — [Phoenix Contact WMTB HF (55X15)R](https://www.phoenixcontact.com/en-sg/products/cable-marker-wmtb-hf-55x15r-0830409)
- Head pressure: patents state there is no standard printhead load for a given media type and that it is tuned by trial and error; more pressure improves contrast but raises platen grip and motor torque and eventually smears, too little lets the media slip; uneven pressure across the head gives uneven density — [US 9902175](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9902175), [US 5206662](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/5206662), [US 6353453](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6353453)
- Card printing: a card-guide patent uses a foam-core roller with fabric-reinforced rubber surface and stresses that adequate head pressure and sufficient roller-to-card friction are needed for image quality and clean advance on card stock — [US 4695850](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/4695850)
- Registration in pocket label printers is selectable between gap, black-mark and continuous media, with cover-open and out-of-paper status bits (sensor hardware not described) — [Fichero PROTOCOL.md](https://github.com/0xMH/fichero-printer/blob/main/docs/PROTOCOL.md)
- In cheap direct-thermal printers the head/platen nip itself pulls the media; there is no separate feed roller — [HackSpace cat printer review](https://hackspace.raspberrypi.com/articles/bluetooth-cat-thermal-printer-review)

### Inferences
- Why rigid tags are harder than tape (engineering reasoning, not sourced): (a) tape conforms to the head over a soft platen, a 0.6-1.0 mm rigid tag does not, so any bow, burr, embossed edge or thickness variation gives voids; (b) the leading edge of a stiff tag is a step that the head must climb, which wrinkles the ribbon and wears the head's protective glaze, so the head should close on the tag after it has entered or the tag needs a chamfer/lead-in shoe; (c) a thick plastic tag is a bigger heat sink than thin liner-backed label film, so more energy per dot and slower speed are needed; (d) the last few millimetres cannot be printed while the tag still needs to be gripped, so either a feed roller pair before and after the head or an unprintable margin of roughly the nip-to-roller distance (5-10 mm) is needed; (e) tags with holes/slots for cable ties interrupt platen support and confuse reflective sensors.
- Candidate mechanisms for the pocket form: (1) straight-through pinch feed: tag enters one side and exits the other, driven by the platen roller under the head (as in cat printers) plus one idler pair; simplest and smallest, margin-limited. (2) Sled/shuttle: tag is clipped in a thin carrier that the platen drives; solves flatness, edges and margins and mirrors the plate concept of the MG3, at the cost of a loose part and a longer path (sled must protrude on both sides). (3) Reciprocating feed (feed in, print while reversing out of the same slot): halves the envelope needed around the device but needs accurate bidirectional registration.
- Registration: a reflective IR sensor (e.g. TCRT5000-class or a small SMD reflective interrupter) at the slot detects the leading edge; steps from edge to heater line are then counted. A transmissive fork sensor is more robust on yellow glossy plastic but costs slot depth. A stepper (open-loop step count) makes registration trivial; a DC motor needs an encoder, as Brother does.
- Planning value for head load, by analogy with 2-inch mechanisms that are commonly specified in the range of a few newtons per centimetre of head width: about 3-6 N on a 12 mm head. This is an unsourced estimate; build an adjustable spring and test.
- A compliant platen (silicone, roughly 30-50 Shore A, 8-10 mm diameter) is needed to make a rigid tag conform to the heater line; smaller diameters increase local pressure but need more torque and wear faster.

### Gaps
- No quantitative head-pressure, platen hardness or flatness-tolerance data for thermal transfer on rigid markers was found in datasheets; the patents are qualitative.
- No documentation of the Weidmüller PrintJet (inkjet with fixation) or Partex feed mechanisms was retrieved in this session.
- No example of any commercial device that pinch-feeds a single loose rigid tag of 12 x 40 mm through a thermal-transfer nip was found; this appears to be unproven territory and the core technical risk of the thermal-transfer route.

## 4. Alternatives to thermal transfer and their fit in the envelope (ranking)

### Takeaway
Thermal inkjet with a 12.7 mm (TIJ 2.5 class) head is the only alternative already proven in the target envelope (Selpic P1: 130 x 31 x 24 mm, 92 g) and needs no contact with the tag, but its durability on PVC/polyolefin and nozzle drying with solvent ink are open risks. Direct thermal and diode-laser marking do not fit the use case; the suggested ranking is (1) thermal transfer resin, (2) solvent TIJ, (3) laser on laser-markable tags, (4) direct thermal, with UV-curable last for lack of small components.

### Cited Findings
- Existence proof for pen-size TIJ: Selpic P1 measures about 130 x 31 x 24 mm, weighs about 86-92 g, has a 210 mAh non-replaceable Li-ion battery, prints 600 x 600 dpi with 300 nozzles and a 12.7 mm print height (max area 12.7 x 500 mm); cartridges cost about USD 39-59 — [MakeUseOf review](https://www.makeuseof.com/selpic-p1-review/), [MBReviews](https://www.mbreviews.com/selpic-p1-review/), [PC Watch](https://pc.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/column/yajiuma-mini-review/1264180.html)
- HP 45 (51645A): TIJ 2.5 technology, 42 ml delivered volume, 300 nozzles, about 33 pl drop size; published "dimensions" of 158 x 117 x 26 mm and 148 g are identical to HP's package dimensions, i.e. they describe the retail box, not the cartridge body — [HP 51645A specifications](https://hp.com/ng-en/products/ink-toner/product-details/product-specifications/69665), [Bechtle-hosted HP datasheet](https://media.bechtle.com/asrc/180712/1c4b3d4ee288fc9434f5175bf56070570/c3/-/f1c3f001cba548a4bb04c0c72ba67c7b/)
- Solvent inks for TIJ 2.5: HP 45si-type solvent cartridges with 12.7 mm print height are sold for coding printers; HSA Systems states that improved solvent inks made previously unprintable materials printable on TIJ 2.5; HP positions TIJ for coding on hard plastics such as PVC, PET, PP and PE — [HSA black solvent inks](https://hsasystems.com/products/consumables-hp45/black-solvent-inks), [PFFC: HP ink solutions](https://pffc-online.com/news/11513-hp-offers-new-ink-solutions-at-pack-expo)
- Decap (uncapped nozzle open time) is ink-dependent: HP's 2590 solvent cartridge is rated up to 1 h uncapped; a third-party solvent ink claims at least 12 h (vendor claim) — [HP 2590 solvent cartridge](https://foodprocessing.com.au/content/packaging-labelling-coding/product/hp-2590-solvent-print-cartridge-940062739), [Alibaba listing](https://www.alibaba.fr/product-detail/Long-Decap-Time-12-7Mm-Black_1601235757532.html)
- Open-source HP45 driver: the ytec3d/yvodehaas HP45 standalone controller V4.02 uses a Teensy 3.5 and TLC59213 high-side drivers on a 12 V supply; the nozzle (VHD) voltage must be calibrated per printhead; each address needs 3 pulses (primitives split 5/5/4); the head's temperature sensing is only indicative; a driver fault can destroy both nozzles and MCU; project is GPL-3.0 and discontinued — [HP45 standalone controller V4.02](https://github.com/yvodehaas/HP45-standalone-controller-V4.02), [Inkjet Commander](https://github.com/yvodehaas/Inkjet-commander)
- Smaller HP cartridge: C6602A (TIJ 1.0) has only 12 nozzles at 96 dpi and a 0.125 in (3.2 mm) swath, 18 ml, body about 44 x 39 x 25 mm, max firing 3 kHz; this is the cartridge used by hobby "InkShield"-type boards — [Levata C6602A](https://www.levata.com/en-gb/hp-c6602a-ink), [HP C6602A specifications](https://www.hp.com/sa-en/products/ink-toner/product-details/product-specifications/43339), [Zylax](https://zylax.com.au/hp-c6602a-black-inkjet-print-cartridge)
- Direct thermal: prints typically stay readable about 6 months, can fade within weeks to months with sunlight or heat (within days in direct sun per one vendor), and 1-5 years only in cool, dark, dry storage; thermal transfer with resin ribbon on synthetic stock lasts years outdoors (vendor sources, figures vary) — [HPRT](https://www.hprt.com/Product/Label-Printers/do-direct-thermal-labels-fade-in-sunlight.html), [Labelmetrics](https://labelmetrics.co.uk/blogs/news/how-long-will-thermal-labels-last-a-guide-to-durability-and-lifespan), [Lowry Solutions](https://lowrysolutions.com/blog/thermal-transfer-vs-direct-thermal-label-printing)
- Laser marking: industrial cable/plastic marking uses UV, green or fibre lasers; a 5 W green marking laser is a Class 4 system that only becomes Class 1 when integrated in an enclosure; handheld fibre markers are 20-50 W; a 500 mW blue (450 nm class) handheld marker is advertised for cardboard, wood, leather and "plastic" without cable-tag data — [Keyence cable marking](https://www.keyence.com/products/marker/laser-marker/applications/laser-marking/laser-marking-for-cables-and-fiber-optic-assemblies.jsp), [Gravotech green laser](https://www.gravotech.us/products/integrable-laser-markers/green-laser), [FindLight 500 mW blue marker](https://findlight.net/laser-material-processing/laser-engravers/handheld-laser-markers/500mw-sharp-blue-light-mini-laser-marking-machine-pedb-100b), [Fonon handheld fibre](https://www.fonon.us/?p=2810)

### Inferences
- Ranking for this use case:
  1. Thermal transfer, resin ribbon: the method the whole cable-marker industry uses on these exact PVC/PO tags (Cembre, Phoenix Contact, Partex, Brady), so durability is the least risky. Costs: contact process, ribbon mechanics, and the unproven single-rigid-tag feed.
  2. Solvent thermal inkjet (12.7 mm head): non-contact, so rigid tags, thickness variation and holes are irrelevant; smallest mechanics; proven pen-size. Risks: nozzle drying after hours/days in a pocket (needs a sealing cap and purge/spit routine), wet ink for a few seconds on a non-porous tag (exit path must not touch the print), adhesion and abrasion/chemical resistance on low-surface-energy polyolefin, solvent cartridges are proprietary/chipped and expensive, cold-weather viscosity, and 12.7 mm swath limits tag width to about 12 mm of print.
  3. Diode laser on laser-markable tags: no consumable, permanent mark; but standard yellow PVC/PO tags are not formulated for 450 nm diode marking, the beam must be scanned (galvo or XY stage, bulky and slow), PVC releases HCl fumes when burned, and any open slot around a multi-hundred-milliwatt beam is a Class 4 enclosure problem. Not credible in a pocket product without special tags and an interlocked, light-tight drawer.
  4. Direct thermal: mechanically ideal (no ribbon), but it needs heat-sensitive coated media, which rigid yellow cable markers are not, and it fades. Only viable if the product concept changes to adhesive direct-thermal labels stuck onto tags.
  5. UV-curable inkjet: would give the best ink durability on plastic, but no pocket-size head plus UV-LED pinning solution was found; treat as not available off the shelf.
- A hybrid worth noting: Weidmüller's PrintJet family uses inkjet with heat fixation for rigid markers on the desktop, which is indirect evidence that ink on rigid polyamide/PO tags needs a fixation step to be durable; a pocket device cannot afford a fuser.
- The HP 45 cartridge body, from memory, is roughly 75-80 mm x 50 mm x 19-20 mm (about 70-75 cm3). This is unsourced; measure a cartridge before using it in CAD. If correct, a full HP 45 cartridge alone uses half of the volume budget and exceeds a 30-40 mm round cross-section in one axis; Selpic-style proprietary low-volume cartridges are much smaller.

### Gaps
- Bare HP 45 cartridge dimensions, nozzle firing voltage and pulse width from HP OEM documentation were not retrieved (the HP "Extended TIJ" OEM datasheets exist at rbs.rdsoft.ch but values were not extractable).
- No independent durability test of solvent TIJ ink on PVC or halogen-free polyolefin cable markers (rub, isopropanol, oil, UV) was found; HP's substrate claims are marketing-level.
- No data on "TIJ 1/4 inch", Funai or Lexmark-based small cartridges was found in this session.
- No source quantified the diode-laser power needed to mark laser-markable polyolefin tags, nor confirmed that 450 nm works on yellow tags.
- Nothing found on pocket-size UV-curable printing.

## 5. Electronics: MCU/SoC with BLE, motor drive, print-head drive, boost conversion, peak currents

### Takeaway
The electronics are low-risk and small: any BLE SoC (ESP32-C3 module 13 x 12.5 mm, nRF52, or the Jieli-class chips used in Chinese printers) can clock a 96-dot head over SPI-like DATA/CLK/LATCH/STROBE lines. The real design problem is peak power: a 96-dot head at 7-9 V can demand several amps, which a small Li-ion cell cannot supply without strobe splitting, a bulk capacitor, or running the head at cell voltage with longer pulses.

### Cited Findings
- ESP32-C3-MINI-1U module size: 13.2 x 12.5 x 2.4 mm — [ESP32-C3-MINI-1 datasheet](https://cdn.sparkfun.com/assets/b/6/0/3/6/esp32-c3-mini-1_datasheet_en.pdf)
- Chinese mini printers use Bluetooth SoCs from JL (Jieli) rather than Espressif/Nordic parts (GB01/GB02 cat printers) — [catprinter repo (mirror)](https://github.aipraa306.workers.dev/JJJollyjim/catprinter)
- The AiYin D11s exposes four equivalent BLE GATT services (including 0x18F0 with write 0x2AF1 / notify 0x2AF0, and 0xFF00 with 0xFF02/0xFF01) plus classic SPP, uses ESC/POS-style raster (GS v 0, 12 bytes per row, 240 rows for a 30 mm label), three density levels, and a paper-type command (gap / black mark / continuous); its app SDK is the white-label LuckPrinter SDK covering 159+ printer models from four manufacturers — [Fichero PROTOCOL.md](https://github.com/0xMH/fichero-printer/blob/main/docs/PROTOCOL.md), [fichero-printer repo](https://github.com/0xMH/fichero-printer)
- Head electrical interface levels: low-voltage mechanisms run the head at 4.2-8.5 V (Fujitsu FTP-628MCL101) or 4.75-9.5 V with 3.0-3.6 V logic (Seiko LTPD245); catalogue POS heads use 24 V — [Fujitsu datasheet](https://www.mouser.com/datasheet/2/164/ftp_628mcl101_21_50-1149342.pdf), [SII LTPD245](https://sii.co.jp/sps/eg/product/lowvoltage/ltpd245_ltpd345.html), [ROHM KD2003](https://www.rohm.com/products/printheads/pos-terminals/kd2003-f0gb00a-product)
- Peak head current scales with the number of simultaneously driven dots: about 3.4-3.7 A at 9.5 V with 64 dots on for Seiko 2/3-inch mechanisms — [CAPD345A-E datasheet](https://elcodis.com/parts/5352769/CAPD345A-E_p2.html), [Seiko mechanism datasheet](https://store.comet.bg/download-file.php?id=2022)
- Motor options seen in the field: brushed DC motor with photo-interrupter encoder (Brother PT-E300), DC motor with constant-speed regulator PCB (PT-80), stepper driving tape and ribbon together (PT-9200PC) — [Experimental Engineering](https://www.experimental-engineering.co.uk/tag/dc-motor/), [PT-9200PC service manual](https://archive.org/stream/printermanual-brother-pt-9200pc-service-manual/brotherpt-9200pcservicemanual_djvu.txt)
- N20-class micro metal gearmotor: 10 x 12 mm gearbox cross-section, 3 mm D-shaft 9 mm long, about 10 g; high-power 6 V versions stall at about 1.5-1.6 A and should be run at 25 % or less of stall current — [Pololu micro metal gearmotors](https://www.pololu.com/file/0J1487/pololu-micro-metal-gearmotors.pdf)
- ESP32-based open projects so far drive complete serial (TTL/ESC-POS) printer modules rather than a bare head: lnbits tpos-receipt-printer uses an ESP32-C3 with a 58 mm TTL printer; DREi "inkless" drives a QR204 (384 dots, 203 dpi) over UART; the Hackaday "pico-printer" project aims at an open ESP8266/ESP32 control board for bare 58 mm heads (early stage) — [tpos-receipt-printer](https://github.com/lnbits/tpos-receipt-printer), [inkless](https://github.com/DREi-studio/inkless), [pico-printer](https://hackaday.io/project/162315-pico-printer)
- HP45 inkjet drive needs a 12 V rail, high-side driver ICs (TLC59213) and per-head voltage calibration — [HP45 standalone controller](https://github.com/yvodehaas/HP45-standalone-controller-V4.02)

### Inferences
- Typical bare-head interface (generic to these heads; confirm against the chosen head's pinout): DATA-IN, CLK, /LATCH, one to six /STROBE lines, thermistor (about 30 kohm NTC to an ADC), VH and logic supply. 96 dots = 12 bytes per line; SPI at 1-4 MHz is ample. Energy control = strobe on-time scaled by supply voltage (V^2), head temperature (thermistor) and optionally previous-line history. A hardware or timer-enforced strobe timeout is mandatory, because a stuck strobe burns the head within about a second.
- Power architecture options: (A) single cell, head driven directly at 3.0-4.2 V: no boost, peak about 2.1 A with all 96 dots on (166 ohm class), long strobes (several ms), so print speed drops to roughly 5-10 mm/s; only works if the head's element resistance is low enough, to be checked on the real head. (B) single cell plus 7-9 V boost (e.g. TPS61088/MT3608-class) with strobe split into 4 groups of 24 dots: about 1.1 A at 7.4 V = 8 W, about 2.5-3 A from the cell at 85 % efficiency, plus a 470-1000 microfarad low-ESR capacitor to shave pulses. (C) two cells in series (2S) feeding the head directly at 6-8.4 V: best electrically, but doubles battery volume and complicates USB-C charging (needs a boost charger such as BQ25883-class).
- Text at typical 15-25 % coverage rarely fires more than 30-50 % of dots in one line, but solid bars (inverse text, barcodes) do; firmware must cap simultaneous dots regardless of content.
- Motor driver: a single DRV8833/DRV8837-class H-bridge handles an N20 gearmotor or a small bipolar stepper (two bridges). A stepper gives open-loop registration; a 15 mm can-stack stepper with reduction is the conventional choice in 58 mm mechanisms, while N20 plus encoder is smaller in cross-section.
- SoC choice: ESP32-C3/C6 is the pragmatic choice for AI-assisted development (toolchain, examples, BLE plus OTA); nRF52832/840 has much lower radio current and better BLE stack, relevant only for standby life. Wi-Fi transmit bursts on ESP32-class chips are in the hundreds of milliamps and should not coincide with head strobes; BLE-only operation avoids that.

### Gaps
- ESP32-C3 and nRF52 current figures were not confirmed from datasheets in this session (search excerpts did not include the tables).
- The actual SoC, motor driver and head-drive topology of Niimbot B18/D11 and Phomemo devices remain unidentified (no teardown found).
- No open firmware that drives a bare thermal print head (strobe/latch/thermistor energy control) with documented timing was located; the cited projects use printer modules with their own controller.

## 6. Battery: cell formats, capacity, peak discharge, labels per charge, charging and protection

### Takeaway
A single 14500 (about 800 mAh, 3 Wh, up to 32 g) or 16340/18350 cell fits the envelope and should give a few hundred tags per charge based on the Brady M211 benchmark (about 300 labels from 1200 mAh); a 10440 (about 350 mAh) is marginal on both energy and peak current. Peak current of 1.5-3 A, not capacity, is the sizing criterion.

### Cited Findings
- 14500 Li-ion: 800 mAh nominal, weight 32 g or less (Emmerich/Conrad); a Sanyo 14500-size cell is listed at 840 mAh with 1680 mA constant discharge current — [Conrad 14500 datasheet](https://asset.conrad.com/media10/add/160267/c1/-/en/001362894DS01/tehnicni-podatki-1362894-emmerich-14500-specialni-akumulatorji-14500-flat-top-li-ion-37-v-800-mah.pdf), [Sanyo 14500 manual](https://manualshive.com/en/manual/sanyo/cr14500/359589)
- 10440 Li-ion: about 350 mAh for ICR and IMR types; IMR type tested at 3 A (forum test table, 2012) — [BudgetLightForum battery comparison](https://budgetlightforum.com/t/battery-comparison-guide/23822)
- 16340: about 850 mAh nominal, up to a high-drain 1000 mAh cell with 2 A continuous and 19.0 g — [Keeppower cells](https://keeppower.com.cn/products_detail.php?id=809)
- 18350: 950 mAh at 22 +/- 2 g (high-current type); up to 1600 mAh with 3.0 A max continuous and 24.5 g; high-capacity types perform poorly at high current — [BLF Tensai 18350 test](https://budgetlightforum.com/t/test-review-of-tensai-lc18350-950mah-gray/26805), [Keeppower 18350](https://keeppower.com.cn/products_detail.php?id=807), [lygte-info Vapcell 18350](https://lygte-info.dk/review/batteries2012/Vapcell%20INR18350%201400mAh%20F14%20%28Green%29%202021%20UK.html)
- Field benchmarks: AiYin D11s uses one 18500 cell of 1200 mAh charged at 5 V 1 A over USB-C; Niimbot B18 1200 mAh (one listing 1500 mAh); Brady M211 1200 mAh, 4 h recharge, "up to 300 labels per charge"; Selpic P1 inkjet pen 210 mAh — [Fichero PROTOCOL.md](https://github.com/0xMH/fichero-printer/blob/main/docs/PROTOCOL.md), [smartx.bg B18](https://www.smartx.bg/en/collections/vendors/products/prienosim-printier-za-ietikieti-niimbot-b18-bluetooth-1200mah-203dpi-bial), [GoToPAC M211](https://www.gotopac.com/brady-m211.html), [MakeUseOf Selpic P1](https://www.makeuseof.com/selpic-p1-review/)
- Charging limits: Li-ion charge window 0 to 45 C, discharge -20 to 60 C; no charging below freezing because metallic lithium plates on the anode, permanently; some vendors put the lower charge limit at +5 C — [Battery University BU-410](https://batteryuniversity.com/article/bu-410-charging-at-high-and-low-temperatures), [REDARC](https://www.redarcelectronics.com/us/resources/chargers-isolators-faqs/do-not-charge-lithium-battery-below-32-degrees/)
- Brother's lithium pack in the PT-E300 has a third contact, probably for temperature sensing — [Experimental Engineering](https://www.experimental-engineering.co.uk/tag/dc-motor/)

### Inferences
- Cell geometry follows the naming convention (diameter in mm, length in tenths of mm): 10440 = 10 x 44 mm (3.5 cm3), 14500 = 14 x 50 mm (7.7 cm3), 16340 = 16 x 34 mm (6.8 cm3), 18350 = 18 x 35 mm (8.9 cm3), 18500 = 18 x 50 mm (12.7 cm3), 18650 = 18 x 65 mm (16.5 cm3). Protected cells are 2-4 mm longer. An 18650 fits the length but takes about half of a 36 mm cross-section; a flat LiPo pouch (e.g. 6 x 20 x 40 mm, about 400-500 mAh) packs better against a PCB but has lower peak-current capability per mAh unless a high-rate type is chosen.
- Bottom-up energy per 12 x 40 mm tag at 203 dpi: 96 x 320 = 30,720 dots; at 15-25 % coverage about 4,600-7,700 fired dots x 0.3-0.5 mJ = 1.4-3.9 J at the head, about 2-5 J from the battery after conversion; motor about 0.5-1 W for about 4 s = 2-4 J; MCU/BLE about 0.3 W for about 5 s = 1.5 J. Total about 6-11 J per tag while printing.
- Top-down benchmark: Brady M211, 1200 mAh x 3.7 V = 4.4 Wh = 16 kJ for 300 labels = about 53 J per label including standby, Bluetooth idle and larger labels. Real-world planning value: 20-50 J per tag.
- Resulting tags per charge: 10440 (1.3 Wh, 4.7 kJ) about 90-230; 14500 (3.0 Wh, 10.7 kJ) about 210-530; 18350/16340 (3.1-3.5 Wh) about 220-600; 18500 (4.4 Wh) about 320-800. The bottom-up figure suggests more (1000+ on a 14500) if sleep current is well controlled.
- Peak-current check: architecture (B) in section 5 needs about 2.5-3 A bursts from the cell. A standard 14500 (about 1.7 A continuous) needs the bulk capacitor and strobe splitting to stay within rating; an IMR/INR high-drain 14500 or 18350 (3 A or more) is the comfortable choice. A 10440 is only viable with direct low-voltage head drive and slow printing.
- Charger: any single-cell linear USB charger (TP4056, MCP73831, BQ24075-class with power path) at 0.5-1 A, with CC pull-downs (5.1 kohm) on USB-C; add an NTC-based charge inhibit below 0 C (JEITA-capable ICs such as BQ2407x/BQ25180-class) because the device will be left in cold vans; protection via DW01/FS8205-class or a cell with integrated protection PCB; fuel gauge optional (voltage-based is adequate).

### Gaps
- No measured energy-per-label data for any pocket thermal-transfer printer was found; the 20-50 J planning number is derived from one marketing claim.
- Peak discharge ratings for current (2025-2026) 14500 and 10440 high-drain cells were not retrieved from manufacturer datasheets.
- Brother PT-E310BT battery capacity not found.

## 7. Volume budget: thermal-transfer design vs thermal-inkjet design

### Takeaway
A thermal-transfer single-tag printer comes out at roughly 110-165 cm3 and 135-190 g, so it is tight but plausible inside 100-200 cm3 as a flattened bar of about 36-40 x 28-32 x 120-130 mm rather than a true 30 mm round flashlight; a TIJ design with a small proprietary cartridge is realistic at about 100-130 cm3 (the Selpic P1 already ships at 97 cm3), while a design around a full-size HP 45 cartridge is marginal to infeasible. The bottlenecks are the cross-section (ribbon cassette width plus gear side-plate plus walls) and packing efficiency, not length.

### Cited Findings
- Commercial envelopes for comparison: Niimbot B18 137 x 90 x 37 mm, 310 g (thermal transfer, label roll inside); Brady M211 about 137 x 102 x 66 mm, 0.54 kg; Selpic P1 inkjet pen 130 x 31 x 24 mm, 86-92 g — [scom.hu B18](https://www.scom.hu/Portable-Label-Printer-Niimbot-B18-White), [RS Brady M211](https://uk.rs-online.com/web/p/label-printers/2425246), [MBReviews Selpic P1](https://www.mbreviews.com/selpic-p1-review/)

- Packing efficiency (60-65 %) and wall-volume figures are generic consumer-product rules of thumb, not sourced; a CAD block model is needed to confirm.

## 8. Environmental robustness for construction-site use

### Takeaway
The commercial benchmark (Brady M211) is rated for 1.8 m drops, 115 kg crush and MIL-STD-810G shock but publishes no IP rating, which reflects the reality that an open media slot and print nip cannot be sealed; the practical targets are drop survival via over-moulding, a slot shutter or cap against dust, and firmware temperature limits (no Li-ion charging below 0 C).

### Cited Findings
- Brady M211: tested to withstand a 6 ft (1.8 m) drop, 250 lb (115 kg) crush force and military-grade shock per MIL-STD-810G — [Brady M211 product page](https://www.bradyid.com/label-printers/portable/m211-portable-bluetooth-label-printer-pid-170380), [Brady EU](https://www.brady.eu/label-printers/m211-printer-emea-pid-m211-eu-uk-us)
- The MIL-STD-810G shock/vibration reference cited for the Brady M210 family is Method 516.6 (listed as "5.16.6 SA4.6.5") — [Brady/Zygology M210-M211 brochure](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0264/6871/2482/files/Brady_Zygology_M210_M211_Mobile_Printers.pdf)
- Li-ion: charge only between 0 and 45 C; discharge is permitted from -20 to 60 C; charging below 0 C causes permanent lithium plating even though the pack appears to charge normally; capacity drops in the cold — [Battery University BU-410](https://batteryuniversity.com/article/bu-410-charging-at-high-and-low-temperatures)
- Tag materials themselves are rated far wider than the printer needs to be: Partex halogen-free markers -40 to +90 C — [Partex brochure 2024](https://sm-strojkoplast.si/uploads/2024/03/Partex-brosura-2024.pdf)
- Ribbons: specialty resin grades exist for prints that must survive cold-storage environments (this concerns the printed image, not the temperature at which printing occurs) — [Ricoh ribbon types](https://www.rei.ricoh.com/newsroom-blog/understanding-resin-wax-resin-and-wax-ribbons/)
- HP's TIJ cartridge specifications include operating humidity limits (10-90 % RH for C6602A), i.e. ink cartridges carry their own environmental limits — [HP C6602A specifications](https://www.hp.com/sa-en/products/ink-toner/product-details/product-specifications/43339)

### Inferences
- Dust is the main killer of thermal heads on site: grit between head and ribbon/tag scratches the glaze and produces permanent white lines. Mitigations: a spring-loaded shutter or tethered cap over the tag slot, a felt wiper at the slot entry, the ribbon itself acting as a sacrificial layer between tag and head (an advantage of thermal transfer over direct thermal), and a user-replaceable head/platen module. An honest rating would be IP5X with slot closed, and no water rating beyond splash (IPX2-IPX4) unless the USB-C port is sealed or capped.
- For TIJ the equivalents are nozzle-plate contamination and solvent evaporation: a positive-seal cap is mandatory, and dust on wet ink ruins prints.
- Drop: at under 200 g a 1.5-2 m drop onto concrete is survivable with a TPE over-mould, a floating (spring-mounted) head, and no cantilevered heavy parts (the battery must be captured, not soldered by tabs only). The ribbon cassette latch and the platen pivot are the likely weak points.
- Cold: at 0 to -10 C the head must add energy (thermistor compensation), resin transfer becomes harder and ribbon/PET becomes more brittle, tags stiffen, Li-ion internal resistance rises so peak current capability drops just when more energy is needed. Design rule: print allowed down to about -10 C with slower speed, charging blocked below 0 C, and recommend keeping the device in an inside pocket (which the form factor already implies). Solvent TIJ inks thicken in the cold and may not fire reliably; this is an additional argument for thermal transfer on outdoor sites.
- Heat: a device left on a dashboard can exceed 60 C; resin ribbon tolerates this better than wax; Li-ion must not be charged above 45 C.

### Gaps
- No IP rating for any pocket thermal-transfer label printer was found; Brady M211 operating temperature range was not retrieved.
- No data on minimum ambient temperature for reliable resin-ribbon transfer onto PVC/PO was found; needs a climate-chamber or freezer test.
- No drop-test data for Niimbot/Phomemo-class devices.

## 9. Open-source projects, reverse-engineered protocols, and prototyping by modifying an existing device

### Takeaway
The app/protocol side is well covered by open source (NiimBlue/niimbluelib and niimprint for Niimbot including the B18, fichero-printer for the AiYin D11s, phomemo-tools, Brother P-touch tools), so a first proof of principle can be done by feeding real tags (on a carrier) through an unmodified or lightly modified Niimbot B18 and printing from open tools. No open firmware for driving a bare 12 mm head was found, so the custom device will need its own firmware.

### Cited Findings
- NiimBlue (web client) and niimbluelib (MIT) are described as the most accurate open implementation of the Niimbot protocol; protocol notes are at niim-docs.pages.dev and a per-model hardware wiki at printers.niim.blue; the project also has a firmware flasher and a collection of firmware images; Android/iOS apps are experimental — [NiimBlue wiki](https://github.com/MultiMote/niimblue/wiki), [niimbluelib README](https://unpkg.com/@mmote/niimbluelib@0.0.1-alpha.38/README.md)
- Python niimprint fork tested on Niimbot B1, B18, B21, D11, D110; NiimPrintX lists D11, D11_H, D101, D110, D110_M, B1, B18, B21 — [AndBondStyle/niimprint](https://www.github.com/AndBondStyle/niimprint), [NiimPrintX write-up](https://hamradio.my/?p=8384)
- fichero-printer: full BLE protocol for the AiYin D11s (96 px, 203 dpi) reverse-engineered from the decompiled Android app, with Python CLI, web GUI (ported from NiimBlue) and docs/PROTOCOL.md; raster printing uses ESC/POS GS v 0; firmware-update protocol untested — [fichero-printer](https://github.com/0xMH/fichero-printer), [Hackaday coverage, March 2026](https://hackaday.com/2026/03/09/reverse-engineering-the-bluetooth-fichero-thermal-label-printer-protocol)
- The D11s app is built on the LuckPrinter white-label SDK supporting 159+ printer models across four manufacturers, so the same ESC/POS-like protocol applies to many generic mini printers — [fichero-printer](https://github.com/0xMH/fichero-printer)
- Phomemo: phomemo-tools (CUPS drivers/protocol for M02 and others) and a Phomemo D30 tool exist — [phomemo-tools](https://github.com/taylorjdlee/phomemo-tools), [phomemo-d30](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Fcrabdancing%2Fphomemo-d30)
- Brother P-touch: open tools exist for PT-1230 and the QL series — [pt1230](https://github.com/cbdevnet/pt1230/blob/master/README.md), [brother_ql](https://git.labolyon.fr/epickiwi/brother_ql/raw/commit/40a1badd6623e8b9cd671c73f1cc9008ce35392f/README.md)
- Cat printers (GB01, GB02, GB03, GT01, MX05, MX06, MX08, MX10; 384 px): BLE protocol reverse-engineered, several clients — [NaitLee/Cat-Printer fork](https://github.com/urufusan/cat-printer), [WerWolv blog](https://werwolv.net/blog/cat_printer)
- A curated list of thermal label printer projects exists — [thermal-label "see also"](https://thermal-label.github.io/see-also)
- Inkjet: HP45 open controller hardware and PC software (GPL-3.0, discontinued) — [HP45 standalone controller V4.02](https://github.com/yvodehaas/HP45-standalone-controller-V4.02), [Inkjet Commander](https://github.com/yvodehaas/Inkjet-commander); an HP45-based SMD tape printer project shows printing on a narrow moving plastic strip — [PJRC: HP45 SMD tape printer](https://www.pjrc.com/hp45-smd-tape-printer/)
- 58 mm mechanisms for bench experiments: Fujitsu FTP-628 family (384 dots; MCL121/123 listed at 67.2 x 32 x 11.2 mm) and clones — [Made-in-China listing](https://qijiprinter.en.made-in-china.com/product/SwmtDCkAnrcj/China-58mm-2-Inch-Original-Thermal-Printer-Head-Mechanism-FUJITSU-FTP-628-MCL121-FTP-628-MCL123.html)

### Inferences
- Recommended prototype ladder (cheapest risk reduction first):
  1. Buy a Niimbot B18 (thermal transfer, 12 mm head, 16 m ribbon cassette) and print via niimprint/NiimBlue on real yellow PVC and halogen-free PO tags taped or clipped onto a liner/carrier strip that mimics a 14 mm label. This answers the two biggest questions at once: does the stock ribbon adhere to the tag materials, and does a 0.6-1.0 mm rigid part pass the nip without voids. Expect to defeat the label RFID/gap sensing (NiimBlue exposes label types) or to stick the tags onto genuine label liner.
  2. Tear the B18 down: identify head (markings, pin count, resistance), motor, gear train, ribbon clutch and SoC; measure head load with a spring scale; log head supply voltage and strobe timing with a scope. This fills most gaps in sections 1, 2 and 5.
  3. Build a bench rig with the salvaged head, a silicone platen, slit resin ribbons of several grades, and an ESP32-C3 driving DATA/CLK/LATCH/STROBE directly, to map energy versus print quality versus speed on each tag material and at low temperature.
  4. Only then design the compact mechanism (cassette, straight-through slot or sled) in CAD.
- For a TIJ proof of principle, a Selpic-type pen or any 12.7 mm handheld coder with quick-dry solvent ink can be swiped over tags in a jig to test adhesion and decap behaviour before investing in HP45 driver electronics.
- Reusing a Niimbot/AiYin main board in the final device is unattractive: firmware is closed, consumables are chip-locked, and the feed logic assumes gap labels. Reusing the mechanical parts (head, platen, gears, ribbon spools) around an own ESP32/nRF board is the efficient path. Reusing the open protocols on the phone side (e.g. emulating the simple ESC/POS-style GS v 0 raster service of the D11s) would let existing open clients print to the new device from day one.
- The D11s/Fichero chassis is direct thermal (no ribbon) and therefore only useful as a donor for head/platen/motor or for protocol reference, not for ribbon tests.

### Gaps
- No public teardown or schematic of Niimbot B18/D11, AiYin D11s or Phomemo label printers identifying the SoC was found; whether custom firmware can be flashed onto Niimbot hardware (as opposed to stock images via the NiimBlue flasher) is not documented in what was retrieved.
- No open-source firmware with bare-head energy control was found; this must be written from the head datasheet/measurements.
- Adafruit/SparkFun bare mechanism documentation (Seiko LTP series guides) was not retrieved in this session.
- I found no evidence (as of November 2026) of an existing commercial pocket printer that takes single loose rigid cable tags; absence of evidence here is weak, since searches were English-only and summary-based.
